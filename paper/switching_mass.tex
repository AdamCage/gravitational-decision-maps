\subsection{How much probability switches?}
\label{sec:switching-mass}

Let $C_a$ denote the strict cell of affine score $a$. For two adjacent
cells let $\Gamma_{ab}=\Gamma_{ba}$ be their common open facet and let
$n_{ab}$ be its unit normal pointing from $C_a$ to $C_b$. Write
$v_{ab}(z)=n_{ab}^{\top}F(z)$ and $[v]_+=\max(v,0)$. An absent facet
has zero surface integral. The following rate depends on the deployment
density $\rho$, which can differ from the density driving the flow.

\begin{theorem}[Endpoint switch mass at short times]
\label{thm:switch-mass}
For a finite affine-score partition as in Section~\ref{sec:flat}, let
$X$ have a compactly supported Lipschitz density $\rho$. For each $a\ne b$,
\begin{equation}
 \Pr\{X\in C_a,\ \Phi_T(X)\in C_b\}
 =T\int_{\Gamma_{ab}}\rho(z)[v_{ab}(z)]_+\,\dd S(z)+O(T^2)
 \quad(T\downarrow0).
 \label{eq:directed-switch-mass}
\end{equation}
Consequently, with each unordered facet counted once,
\begin{equation}
 \Pr\{C(X)\ne C(\Phi_T(X))\}
 =T\sum_{a<b}\int_{\Gamma_{ab}}\rho(z)|v_{ab}(z)|\,\dd S(z)+O(T^2).
 \label{eq:total-switch-mass}
\end{equation}
The result also holds for the complete flow of any $C^1$ autonomous
field. Surfaces in dimension one are counted with counting measure.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take a compact ball containing the support of $\rho$ in its interior.
On a fixed larger ball the velocity and its derivative are bounded, so
uniformly for sufficiently small $T$ the considered trajectories move
by at most $MT$, and $\Phi_T(x)=x+TF(x)+O(T^2)$. Use the same speed bound
$M$ at nearby facet points in this larger ball. Consider all distinct
hyperplanes on which two affine scores are equal. A pair with a nonzero
constant difference has no equality plane and its order is fixed
globally. Inside the compact ball the points within $2MT$ of two
distinct planes have volume $O(T^2)$. Parallel distinct planes have
disjoint strips for small $T$. For nonparallel planes, the two signed
distances give a rank-two affine map with constant Jacobian, so bounding
both distances gives the asserted estimate on the ball. In dimension one
all such strips are disjoint. This exceptional set contributes only
$O(T^2)$ probability because $\rho$ is bounded.

Away from this set, every trajectory that switches must start within
$MT$ of a unique equality plane $H$. All comparisons associated with
other planes are fixed on the $MT$ ball about the starting point. Let
$x=z+sn_{ab}$ with $z$ its orthogonal projection onto $H$. The two sides
locally have either the same winner or the two winners of the facet
containing $z$. There cannot be three winning scores tied on a relatively
open subset of $H$. Three distinct affine functions agreeing on $H$
have ordered normal slopes, and the intermediate slope cannot win
strictly anywhere, contrary to the full-dimensional cell assumption.
The foot $z$ is away from intersections of distinct equality planes.
Thus it belongs to the open facet $\Gamma_{ab}$ whenever the local
winners are $a$ and $b$. The same argument applies to every predicted
switch in the model strips below, which also lie within $MT$ of $H$.

For $|s|\le MT$, the final signed distance from $H$ is
$s+Tv_{ab}(z)+O(T^2)$, uniformly. The ideal starting strip for $a$ to $b$
is $-T[v_{ab}(z)]_+<s<0$. Outside the exceptional set the true and
ideal transition indicators can disagree only if
$|s+Tv_{ab}(z)|\le K T^2$ for a uniform $K$. This adds $O(T^2)$
probability by integrating an interval of length at most $2KT^2$ in
each normal fiber over a bounded facet portion. The same skeleton
estimate justifies integrating the ideal strip over the entire facet,
with the compactly supported density set to zero elsewhere. The normal
coordinate Jacobian is one, and replacing $\rho(z+sn_{ab})$ by $\rho(z)$
costs $O(T^2)$ by the Lipschitz bound. This gives
\eqref{eq:directed-switch-mass}. The flow is a diffeomorphism, so ties
have probability zero at both endpoints. Summing both directions over
the finite set of facets gives \eqref{eq:total-switch-mass}.
\end{proof}

When $\rho$ is positive on a permeable patch, the coefficient in
\eqref{eq:total-switch-mass} is positive. This supplies matching
distribution-specific lower and upper rates as $T$ tends to zero.
Equation~\eqref{eq:facetvelocity} makes the coefficient a collection of
$(d-1)$-dimensional integrals without trajectory integration. A density
supported away from the facets can instead give a zero rate even if
the readout has permeable facets.

\begin{corollary}[Identifiability from population invariance]
Suppose the deployment density is positive almost everywhere on a
neighborhood of a nonempty relatively open patch of every facet.
For the analytic Gaussian flow, zero endpoint switch probability
at any single $T>0$ implies invariance of every strict decision cell.
If the density also satisfies Theorem~\ref{thm:switch-mass} and is
positive on a relatively open patch of each facet itself, a zero
leading coefficient in \eqref{eq:total-switch-mass} implies the
same invariance.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If the time map carries any point of a covered patch strictly off
its supporting plane, continuity gives an open set of inputs on
the opposite side that end across that plane. Their initial cell
is one of the two incident cells. Excluding terminal ties, which
have measure zero by diffeomorphic transport, this gives positive
switch probability. Thus the time map preserves each covered patch
in its supporting plane. Analytic continuation and
Lemma~\ref{lem:sampled-tangency} give tangency to the entire plane.
If instead the leading flux coefficient is zero, continuity and
positivity on a facet patch give zero normal velocity on that patch.
Tangency again extends analytically to each plane and preserves the
strict cells.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{A finite-horizon lower certificate.}
Fix $x\in C_a$ and a bounding facet $H$ of $C_a$. Orient its normal
$n$ toward the exterior of $C_a$ and let $s_0<0$ be the signed
distance of $x$ to $H$. Let $K_T(x)$ be its reachable projected tube in
\eqref{eq:projected-tube}, with the current normal orientation, and put
\[
 U_H=\frac{(\max_i n^\top a_i-\min_i n^\top a_i)^2}{4\beta}-1,
 \qquad \psi(U,T)=\int_0^T e^{-Uu}\,\dd u.
\]
In particular, $\psi(0,T)=T$ and
$\psi(U,T)=(1-e^{-UT})/U$ for $U\ne0$.
If the plane velocity satisfies $n^\top F(o+y)\ge h>0$ on $K_T(x)$,
then the inequality
\begin{equation}
       -s_0<h\,\psi(U_H,T)
       \label{eq:forced-switch-tube}
\end{equation}
guarantees that $\Phi_T(x)$ is strictly outside $C_a$.
Indeed, $\partial_n(n^\top F)\le U_H$ by the scalar posterior variance
bound. While $s(t)\le0$, integrating this derivative from the plane
in the negative normal direction gives $\dot s\ge h+U_Hs$. If the
trajectory stayed nonpositive through $T$, the integrating factor
would give $s(T)\ge e^{U_HT}(s_0+h\psi(U_H,T))>0$. At a contact with
$H$ the derivative is strictly positive, which also excludes return
from its positive side before $T$. The argument guarantees an endpoint
decision change for strict endpoint labels. The destination can be a
third cell, since the rest of the readout may also be traversed.

One implementation chooses the threshold
$h_0=-s_0/\psi(U_H,T)>0$ directly. Using the relative logits $L_i$ and
their extrema over $K_T(x)$ from \eqref{eq:local-plane}, it is
sufficient that
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{i:r_i\ge h_0}(r_i-h_0)e^{\underline L_i}
 +\sum_{i:r_i<h_0}(r_i-h_0)e^{\overline L_i}>0 .
 \label{eq:local-forced-switch}
\end{equation}
This lower-bounds the numerator for the plane velocity minus $h_0$.
Strict positivity on the compact tube yields a uniform $h>h_0$,
and thus \eqref{eq:forced-switch-tube}. A conservative implementation
can use $T\exp(-\max(U_H,0)T/2)$ in place of $\psi(U_H,T)$ by Jensen's
inequality.

\paragraph{A local two-sided bracket.}
Define $\ell_H(x,T)$ and $u_H(x,T)$ as the minimum and maximum of
$\sum_i r_i e^{q_i}/\sum_i e^{q_i}$ over the independent logit intervals
$q_i\in[\underline L_i,\overline L_i]$. These bound the plane velocity
uniformly on $K_T(x)$. Comparing $\ell_H$ with a threshold uses the
sign test \eqref{eq:local-forced-switch}. Comparing $u_H$ uses the
opposite extremum for each term. Let $S_T^{-}$ be the union, over
bounding facets of each query's strict cell, of the queries satisfying
$-s_0<[\ell_H(x,T)]_+\psi(U_H,T)$. Define $S_T^{+}$ by the corresponding
union using $-s_0\le [u_H(x,T)]_+(e^T-1)$. On the initial side of a facet,
$\partial_n(n^\top F)\ge-1$ yields $\dot s\le u_H-s$ up to first contact.
A contact by $T$ then requires $-s_0\le [u_H]_+(e^T-1)$. With
\eqref{eq:forced-switch-tube}, this gives
\begin{equation}
 S_T^-\ \,\subseteq\ \,\{x:C(x)\ne C(\Phi_T(x))\}\ \subseteq\ \, S_T^+
 \qquad\text{up to endpoint ties}.
 \label{eq:local-switch-bracket}
\end{equation}
The upper bound here uses reachable projected tubes in each plane.
Replacing $u_H(x,T)$ by the global group-mean maximum gives the earlier
departure slabs, which can be wider.

For any bounded compactly supported query density, the local bracket
has unresolved probability $\Pr\{X\in S_T^+\setminus S_T^-\}=O(T^2)$.
Indeed, for each fixed facet, $K_T(x)$ has diameter $O(T)$ uniformly
over the support. The relative logits are affine, so their box has
width $O(T)$ and contains the logits at the projected initial point
$z$. The normal posterior mean has bounded derivatives in the logits,
hence $\ell_H(x,T)$ and $u_H(x,T)$ both equal $v_H(z)+O(T)$ uniformly.
Both time factors equal $T+O(T^2)$, including the conservative Jensen
factor. The lower and upper threshold distances both equal
$T[v_H(z)]_++O(T^2)$. A query in $S_T^+\setminus S_T^-$ is therefore in
an interval of normal width $O(T^2)$ about that distance for at least
one bounding plane. Integrating the bounded density over a compact
tangential projection and summing over finitely many planes proves the
claim. In particular, under Theorem~\ref{thm:switch-mass}, the two
certificate masses have the same first-order coefficient as the
actual switch mass. A path that leaves its initial strict cell and
returns by $T$ also belongs to the unresolved set, so its probability
has the same quadratic upper order. The bracket and its rate concern
exact arithmetic.

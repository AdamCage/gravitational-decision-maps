\subsection{Verifying a margin reserve for an arbitrary affine readout}
\label{sec:reserve-verification}

The Voronoi certificates use equal mixture masses and exploit the
fact that the two incident sites cancel exactly on their bisector.
The following variant accommodates an independently trained affine
head and arbitrary positive mixture masses. For an initial strict
winner, write a rival margin as $m(z)=d^\top z+b$ and ask that it
exceed $\gamma m(x)$ throughout $[0,T]$ for $0<\gamma<1$.
If $d=0$, the margin is constant. Otherwise let $\pi(z)$ be the
orthogonal projection onto $m=0$. The projected path lies in
\[
  \{(1-u)\pi(x)+uy: y\in\operatorname{conv}\{\pi(a_i)\}_i,
        \ 0\le u\le 1-e^{-T}\},
\]
by variation of constants. The common quadratic term in the Gaussian
logits cancels, so evaluating each affine logit at $\pi(x)$ and
$\pi(a_i)$ encloses that logit's range on the projected path.

Let $L$ be a lower bound on the posterior mean of $m(a_i)$ on this
projected set. Moving from the projection in the direction of $d$
cannot decrease this posterior mean, by the covariance identity
\eqref{eq:covariance}. Hence while $m(z)\ge0$,
$\dot m\ge L-m$. Direct comparison gives
\[
  m(\Phi_t x)\ge e^{-t}m(x)+(1-e^{-t})L.
\]
The lower bound is affine in $1-e^{-t}$. To certify the reserve, it
suffices to show that $L>m(x)(\gamma-e^{-T})/(1-e^{-T})$. The bound then
exceeds the reserve at both ends and throughout, which rules out a
first violation. We test this strict comparison by bounding the
sign of the weighted exponential numerator, with the same outward
kernels as the Voronoi test. Using $\min_i m(a_i)$ for $L$ gives a
global baseline. The local test uses the projected logit intervals.
We enclose affine products from the encoded readout weights and biases
rather than treating rounded unit normals as exact.

For a straight segment from $x$ to $z$, the endpoint test
$m(z)>\gamma m(x)$ suffices for every point of the segment. We check
it directly with interval dot products. Thus the deployed output can
be verified even when the optimizer or numerical integrator that
proposes it uses ordinary arithmetic. The guarantee concerns the
encoded model and features. It implies neither accuracy of the initial
decision nor global optimality of the numerical projection.
